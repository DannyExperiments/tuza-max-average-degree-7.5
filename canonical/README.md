These chapters give the longer arguments behind the three main theorems and the four-edge reduction. They reproduce selected mathematical records from 20–21 November 2026, including the bounds that the final discharging argument takes as inputs. The formulas, incidence conditions, exceptional configurations and labeled diagrams come from those records. Cross-references point into this release or to the original papers. Throughout, $\nu$ denotes the maximum number of edge-disjoint actual triangles and $\tau$ the minimum number of graph edges meeting every actual triangle. A high vertex has degree at least eight in its specified ambient graph. The counting arguments often reserve “low” for degree at most six and treat sevens separately. When they say a vertex $h$ *subsumes* $v$, they mean $N[v]\subseteq N[h]$. In a subgraph-minimal violation every proper subgraph satisfies Tuza. A *tight* graph satisfies $\tau=2\nu$.

### What the density argument imports

1. The proofs use Puleo’s low-vertex restrictions and discharging, the small-neighborhood lemma of Botler, Fernandes and Gutiérrez, Gupta’s degree-seven pair and rooted-link reductions, and the specified local lemmas of Kandasamy. The chapters identify the applicable link, degree, matching and ambient-extension hypotheses. [Puleo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2211) · [Botler, Fernandes and Gutiérrez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07925) · [Gupta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06538) · [Kandasamy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15269)
2. The threshold changes in three steps. At $127/17$ the largest donor ratio is $17/8$. Redistributing the contribution of a degree-five vertex to its actual high subsumers gives the ratio $132/65$ at $989/132$. At $15/2$ a further transfer of $1/30$ at the exceptional mixed nines gives a pointwise ratio of at most two, strictly less than two at every vertex of degree at least eleven. The total weight remains the number of vertices throughout both redistributions. [Original donor bounds](donor-bounds.md) · [First redistribution](density-989-132.md) · [Final redistribution](density-endpoint.md)
3. Summation yields average degree **at least** $15/2$ in a minimal violation. Kandasamy’s maximum-degree-ten theorem guarantees a vertex of degree at least eleven and makes this inequality strict. The conclusion for $\mathrm{mad}(G)<15/2$ uses the local inputs alone. The inclusive endpoint additionally relies on the global degree-ten theorem and its external computer-assisted proof. Its full certificate suite has not been rerun for this release. [Final sum](density-endpoint.md#endpoint) · [Kandasamy, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15269v1#S1.Thmtheorem1)

### Proof and replay

Numerical comparisons, positive packing/cover witnesses and finite catalogues address the explicitly stated domains. The ambient transfer, the reduction to the finite domains and the quantifiers over graphs are mathematical parts of the arguments. The accompanying verification uses the supplied data for the codegree-four pair witnesses, five- and six-tip checks, marked links, exceptional fan configurations, and counting arithmetic. [Replay instructions](../REPRODUCIBILITY.md) · [Certificate data and attribution](../verification/DATA_SOURCES.md)

The earlier donor checks also replay from the public data. The eight-link classification checks all 12,346 isomorphism classes, including 203 partial-star witnesses and 203 retained classes. The five-mark check then tests 11,368 placements and 363,776 subset prices. The mixed-nine check verifies 64 masks, including the possible exterior exits. The two-five check transports six witnesses across all 25,088 labeled graphs in its supported domain. These finite computations feed the stated structural reductions. The published degree-ten theorem and its full external certificate suite remain an imported input for the inclusive endpoint.
