# The problem and the proof

All graphs are finite and simple. A triangle cover is a set of graph edges meeting every triangle. A triangle packing is a set of triangles with pairwise disjoint **edge sets**. Write $\tau$ and $\nu$ for their minimum and maximum sizes. A graph is tight when $\tau=2\nu$. Define $\operatorname{mad}(G)$ as the maximum of $2|E(J)|/|V(J)|$ over nonempty subgraphs $J\subseteq G$, with value zero for the empty graph. The three main statements apply to all graphs satisfying their respective hypotheses. [Manuscript](paper/manuscript.pdf)

## A. Maximum average degree

If $\operatorname{mad}(G)\le 15/2$, then $\tau(G)\le 2\nu(G)$. To see why the endpoint is a separate step, pass to an inclusion-minimal violating subgraph $M$ with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges. Use $H$ for the vertices of degree at least eight in $M$. The low-degree reductions of Puleo, Botler–Fernandes–Gutiérrez, the certified seven-link and adjacent-seven inputs extending Gupta, and the octahedral six case yield the following accounting. After redistribution, the surplus $s'(v)$ is nonnegative and sums to $2m-7n$. One counting unit is assigned per vertex, distributing each low vertex’s unit among its high neighbors. The adjusted weights on $H$ still sum to $n$.

The difficult row is a degree-nine vertex with four high neighbors subsuming both a five and a nonoctahedral six. Here subsumption of $x$ by $h$ means $N[x]\subseteq N[h]$. Such a nine has adjusted weight at most $11/5$ and surplus at least $13/12$. The two subsumed neighborhoods have a union of size eight. The numbers of highs in the five-link and the six-link can be $(3,4),(3,5),(4,4)$. Partitioning these high neighbors by their unique incident five or six forces a neighboring high outside the exceptional row that subsumes a low. Send $1/30$ of weight to one such neighbor. This leaves at most $13/6$ at the source, and a recipient with $j$ high neighbors receives at most $j/30$. The recipient bounds give $w(h)\le2s'(h)$, strictly at each vertex of degree at least eleven. The finite arithmetic supplements the structural argument establishing eligibility of the recipient. Summing gives $n\le 2(2m-7n)$, hence $2m/n\ge15/2$. This already excludes $\operatorname{mad}(G)<15/2$. Kandasamy’s maximum-degree-ten theorem forces a vertex of degree at least eleven in $M$. One summand is then strict and $2m/n>15/2$, which also excludes equality. The local use of his Lemma 3.5 invokes the connected octahedral link. ## B. A triangle-free high subgraph

Let $H(G)=G[\{v:\deg_G(v)\ge8\}]$. If $H(G)$ is triangle-free, then $\tau(G)\le2\nu(G)$. Every vertex high in a subgraph remains high in the ambient graph. Thus the hypothesis passes to a minimal violation $M$. Low-degree reductions exclude fives and sixes, each of which would force a triangle among its high neighbors. Each remaining nonhigh vertex has degree seven.

A wide edge belongs to at least four triangles. Each seven has at least five wide incident edges. In a triangle-free high subgraph the link argument forces some wide seven–seven edge from each seven. Adjacent-seven reductions exclude codegrees five and six. The codegree-four pair certificates leave three signatures. Two have a high clique, and the remaining one has a high square. All private neighbors of the pair see the square. A private high there would make a high triangle. Propagation therefore produces a connected cubic graph $B$ of sevens, all joined to the four square corners and having no other exterior neighbors. A triangle in $B$ would give a seven–seven edge of codegree at least five. Thus $B$ is triangle-free, with $m_B\ge9$ edges.

Greedily color the edges of $B$ with five colors. Use the four largest matchings to pack triangles at the four distinct square corners. This gives at least $4m_B/5$ triangles. Buying all $m_B$ internal edges and the four square edges costs at most $8m_B/5$. These edges cover every triangle lost on deleting the sevens and the square edges, including outside triangles on those square edges. The residual packing and cover therefore extend within budget, a contradiction. ## C. Two high fans

If $\tau(G)>2\nu(G)$, then $H(G)$ contains at least two distinct seven-edge copies of $K_1\vee P_4$. A copy consists of the four spokes and the three edges of the rim path. Extra edges are allowed. Distinct copies may have the same center or vertex set.

The hypothesis “at most one high fan” passes to subgraphs. The fan-free reduction excludes the zero case in a minimal violation. In the one-fan case call its faces $abx,abc,acz$. They form an entire component of the graph on high triangles with adjacency by a shared edge. The other components are single triangles, books with a common base, or tetrahedra. The classified links and local reductions leave a seven representing two disjoint book bases, with two full tips adjacent to all four endpoints, and at most one additional high edge of the specified type.

Form an auxiliary multigraph on book bases with one edge for each represented seven. The exceptional bases are $U=ab$ and $V=ac$. There is no auxiliary edge between them. On a cycle containing at most one exceptional base, orient the cycle and use at each seven one triangle on its tail base and two triangles on sides of its head book. These and the corresponding purchases yield $3k$ packed triangles for at most $6k$ cover edges on a cycle of length $k$. The two exceptional head choices can both require the same physical edge $bc$. Thus the remaining possible cycles contain both exceptions.

After deleting $U,V$, each tree has at most one attachment to each exception. Let $L,D,J$ count its ordinary vertices of full auxiliary degree one, two and at least three, and $r\le2$ its attachments. Then $J\le L+r-2$. Its root edges number $L+D+J-1+r\le3(L+D)$. Every ordinary degree-one or degree-two base needs four distinct outside witness sevens, one per full-tip side, since otherwise deletion along its incident roots admits the local packing and cover. Across bases these demands use distinct sevens because a witness has at most one such additional edge. Each nonempty tree has $L+D>0$. After summation there are too few roots for the $4(L+D)$ demands. In the all-high residual pieces, book bases, tetrahedron covers, and $ab,ac$ on the fan give the required cover. ## Further results

**Four-edge sufficient condition (open).** Let $L$ be tight and assume all its subgraphs satisfy Tuza. Let $u,a,z,b,y$ be distinct vertices of a path in this order, where $u,z,y$ are pairwise nonadjacent. Other chords are allowed. One minimum cover of $L$ avoids all four path edges. Some maximum packing avoids $ua,az$, and some maximum packing avoids $zb,by$. The open assertion asks whether one maximum packing avoids all four. The project proves that an affirmative answer for every such instance would imply the unrestricted conjecture. In a least counterexample, an edge deletion produces a blocked two-edge path in a tight component. Minimize the packing number of a hereditary tight component with such a path. Deleting both legs drops that number by one and again leaves a tight graph. Uncovered returning pages on the two legs must have distinct tips. Their four old edges satisfy the stated hypotheses by minimality. Avoiding all four together would then admit two returning triangles and exceed the original maximum. [Detailed reduction](canonical/four-edge-implication.md)

**Finite census (computational).** The support of a nonempty triangle component comprises its triangles and exactly their incident edges and vertices. Component adjacency means sharing a graph edge. Up to isomorphism, the only such supports on at most ten vertices satisfying $\tau=2\nu$ are $K_4$ and $K_5$. The November census confirms an earlier July result. Counts in the replay are augmentation instances and may contain repeated isomorphism types. Beyond ten vertices the classification is open. [Census replay and counts](REPRODUCIBILITY.md)
